Research

Questions at the intersection
of data and society.

My research examines how privacy concerns, trust, and technological change influence individual decisions, innovation incentives, and economic growth. I combine theoretical modelling with empirical methods, including survey data analysis, Bayesian model averaging, and machine learning.

Job market paper
Innovation · Growth · Privacy

Data-Driven Innovation and Privacy Concerns in a Schumpeterian Growth Model

With Haiping Zhang and Alexandre Dmitriev

This paper develops a Schumpeterian growth model in which consumer data serve as an input to research and development. Individuals balance the economic returns from sharing their data against privacy concerns. Stronger privacy concerns can reduce data supply, innovation, and long-run growth. The model also identifies circumstances in which market innovation may be either below or above the socially desirable level, suggesting that appropriate data policies depend on the underlying incentives and privacy costs.

Working paper
Behavioural economics · Survey data

Privacy Concerns, Trust, and Monitoring: Evidence from Europe

This project studies the trade-off between privacy and government monitoring using the European Social Survey. It examines how a pandemic-related framing shifts stated preferences toward monitoring, and whether responses differ across individuals. The analysis combines survey-based comparisons with machine-learning methods for heterogeneous effects, highlighting institutional trust as an important dimension of variation in privacy–monitoring attitudes.
